# Wallal 1922, Lick 15-foot cameras: summary of the 1923 paper

**Source.** W. W. Campbell and R. Trumpler, "Observations on the deflection of light in passing through the Sun's gravitational field made during the total solar eclipse of Sept. 21, 1922", *Lick Observatory Bulletin* 11, 41–54 (No. 346). Dated May 1923, issued July 5, 1923 (ADS `1923LicOB..11...41C`). Printed page *p* is page *p* − 40 of the ADS PDF. Quotes are verbatim.

**Error convention.** Every ± is a **probable error**. The paper's own test is "only one of the seven results differing from it by more than its probable error" (p.52).

**Data file.** Table II (92 stars) is transcribed in `wallal1923_table2_displacements.csv`. Its checks are in `wallal1928_notes.md` §2 and §3.10. The 1928 paper (LOB 13, 130) combines these 15-foot results with the five-foot cameras (see `wallal1928_method_and_results.md`).

## Instruments and plates

- **Cameras.** A twin camera of 15 feet focal length with two 5-inch doublet objectives, AB and CD. Ratio of aperture 1:36 (p.43). The guiding telescope had a visual 4-inch objective of 15 feet focal length. The guide stars were β Virginis (eclipse field) and 70 Ophiuchi (check field) (p.43).
- **Plates.** "17x17 inch plate glass plates, ¼ inch thick, with Seed 30 emulsion" (p.43). Scale 45″.075 per mm for objective AB and 45″.30 for CD (p.47).
- **Wide field.**
  > "A large field is not only desirable in order to increase the number of stars on the plates, but it is most important for a good determination and elimination of the scale value of the plates." (p.42) … "It is thus possible to separate the two effects by a least squares solution, and the separation will be the more complete the larger the field of the plate." (p.42)
- **Check field.** Centred at R.A. 18h5m.5, Decl. +1°1′, "following the eclipse star field by 6h14m.9, but at the same declination". It was photographed on the same plates as the eclipse field (p.42).
- **Few bright stars.**
  > "The star field of the 1922 eclipse is very poor in bright stars, only two stars brighter than ninth magnitude being situated within 1° from the Sun's center, whereas at the 1919 eclipse seven stars brighter than sixth magnitude were found in a similar field." (p.42)

  The paper also notes that "the 2-minute exposures during the eclipse show as many faint stars as the 3-minute exposures at night in Tahiti. Stars down to the photographic magnitude 10.5 have measurable images." (p.43)
- **Tahiti.** Comparison plates were taken May 7–20, 1922, with 3-minute exposures. The eclipse field was taken at 9h40m–10h30m p.m. and the check field at 4h–4h50m a.m. on the same plates (p.44).
- **Wallal** (p.45):
  > "The first exposure of 2 minutes was started 10 seconds after the beginning of totality; 50 seconds were required to change the plate holders and steady the telescope from the resulting vibrations. The second pair of plates with an exposure of 2 minutes 5 seconds, stopped 10 seconds before the end of totality, received the impression of the check field on the evening of the eclipse day."
- **Weights** (p.45): good 1, fair .7, poor/faint/diffuse .4, very poor/very faint/very diffuse .25.

"May 15, 16h23m.5" is therefore the morning of May 16 (the check field was exposed "at 4h to 4h50m in the morning", p.44), and "Sept. 21, 1h31m.2" at Wallal is 1:31 p.m.

## Measurement and reduction (pp.45–47)

- **Measurement.** Every eclipse and night plate was measured against an intermediate plate (CD, 4-minute exposure at Tahiti), "following in this the example of the Greenwich expedition of 1919" (p.45).
  > "The first three pairs of plates of TABLE I were measured independently by Campbell and Trumpler, the last one only by Trumpler." (p.46)

  The paper reports "Over 50,000 bisections of star images" and 87 check stars (p.46). Screw values were 7″.18 (CD22, CD23), 7″.30 (AB18) and 7″.32 (AB17) (p.47).
- **Corrections.** E − N was corrected for proper motion, parallax, differential refraction and aberration, and plate inclination, "Terms of the second order in x and y had to be included in these corrections" (p.47).
- **Check field.** It was first fitted with all second-order terms (12 constants). In the end only inclination terms were kept: "f = 0, d′ = 0, d = e′ = p, e = f′ = q". This gave 6 constants for CD22 and CD23, 8 for AB18 and 10 for AB17 (p.47).
- **Eclipse field: second-order terms** were taken from the check field. **Linear terms (zero point, orientation, scale)** came from the reference stars beyond 2° (p.47):
  > "The second desideratum could be secured, thanks to the large field of the plate, by using the stars more distant than 2° from the Sun's center (28 to 38) as reference stars for the determination of the linear correction terms (zero point, orientation and scale). Neglecting the light deflections of these stars (0″.14 to 0″.23 according to Einstein's theory) would cause a sensible error only in the scale value thus determined." (p.47)

  "Turner's formula" (6 constants) was used. For CD22 and CD23 the final form had 4 constants (Δx = a + rx + sy, Δy = a′ − sx + ry). AB17 and AB18 kept 6 constants (p.47).
- **Relative displacements** (p.48):
  > "We shall call them the relative displacements, because they are derived by comparing the stars near the Sun with the reference stars of the outer portions of the plate, and the plate constants are so determined as to make the average displacements of the reference stars zero. To convert the relative values into absolute ones an additional scale correction is needed."
- **Weighting.** "the weight of any measure made only by one observer (plate AB17) was reduced by multiplication with 0.9" (pp.48–49). "The four plates were then combined according to their weights" (p.49).

## Table II and the solution (pp.49–52)

Table II lists **92 stars**; Fig. 1 is a "Star chart … containing the 92 stars actually measured" (p.48). Its columns are:

- number, B.D. and photographic magnitude;
- X, Y and the distance from the Sun's centre, "expressed in units of 100 mm. = 4508″" (p.50);
- the mean relative displacements Δx, Δy and the total weight;
- the radial component D₁ ("positive if away from the Sun", p.50);
- D₂, the radial component corrected with the check-star curves;
- the relative Einstein theory, "derived from them by the scale correction: −″.079 d (d = distance from Sun's center). This coefficient is the mean of the scale corrections of the four plates as obtained from the least squares solutions mentioned below." (p.51)

One identification in Table II is inconsistent: star 101 is printed "+1 2639", but its X, Y fit BD +1°2640, the number the 1928 paper prints for the same star (1928 Table 4, No. 99). It is kept as printed (`wallal1928_notes.md` §6).

> "Adopting Einstein's prediction that the displacements are inversely proportional to the distance d from the Sun's center, and keeping in mind that the scale value needs correction, the radial displacements (Δr) observed are represented by a formula Δr = αd + β R/d where: α = correction of scale value, β = light deflection at Sun's limb, R = radius of Sun's disk = 0°.2661. For each plate and each set of measures α and β were determined from the measured radial displacements of all stars by least squares solutions." (p.51)

So, as in 1928, the **scale correction α and the limb deflection β were solved simultaneously** for each plate and observer, using all the stars of the plate.

**Check-star-corrected solution** (p.54):

> "a new least squares solution with the formula of page 51, using the corrected data D₂ of TABLE II, gives for the deflection at the Sun's limb: 2″.05. This result is 17% in excess of the prediction, but it should be kept in mind that it is derived by extrapolation. … It is however inadvisable to pursue this problem any further at the present moment, as the plates of the 5-foot cameras, with a larger field, will shortly supply material much better suited to its investigation." (p.54)

In 1928 (LOB 13, p.152) the authors adopted the **directly observed D₁**, "which had always been considered by the observers as the final results of this instrument". D₂ "were merely given for an estimate of the possible influence of certain systematic errors". The five-foot check field showed none of the 15-foot check-field pattern.

## Use in the 1928 combination

The 1928 paper combines 1″.72 ± ″.11 (weight 2) with the five-foot 1″.82 ± ″.15 (weight 1) to get **1″.75 ± ″.09**. It also merges the two instruments star by star in Tables 10–12. The 15-foot D₁ are put on the five-foot system with a scale correction of +0″.060 per degree, and the five-foot weights are divided by 3. The transcribed Table II reproduces every 15-foot weight and D₁ that enters the 1928 Tables 10 and 12 (`wallal1928_notes.md`).
