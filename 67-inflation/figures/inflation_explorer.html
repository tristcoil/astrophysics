What the two numbers mean

ns measures how the strength of the primordial ripples changes with scale: 1 would be the same on every scale; inflation predicts slightly less than 1 because the field is slowly rolling. r compares gravitational waves with density ripples. Its size fixes the energy at which inflation happened, V1/4 ≈ 1.02×1016 GeV × (r/0.01)1/4.

Why N* is a slider

Which point of the potential we see in the CMB depends on how long ago, in e-folds of expansion, our scales left the horizon. That depends on how the universe reheated after inflation, which we do not know: a model is a track, not a point. Faster or more radiation-like reheating gives larger N*; only an exotic 'stiff' phase pushes N* well beyond 60.

What the verdict means

For each data set we find the smallest region of the ns–r plane holding a given share of the posterior that still contains the model's point, and express that share as Gaussian σ: inside the 68% region is "1σ", the 95% region "2σ". Below 2σ: allowed; 2–3σ: under pressure; beyond 3σ: ruled out.
