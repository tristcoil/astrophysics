Unroll a planet-forming disc

ALMA sees each disc tilted, as an ellipse. Tilt it back (inclination) and turn it (position angle) until the rings are circles: then the unrolled image on the right shows every ring as a straight horizontal stripe, and the brightness profile is at its sharpest. Real DSHARP and HL Tau images; the score below drops as the geometry improves.

Weigh the planet that could carve a gap

Image brightness is shown on an asinh stretch, and the profile and score are computed from those stretched values, so they are qualitative. Planet masses follow Zhang et al. 2018 (DSHARP VII): Δ = A K′B, K′ = q (h/r)−0.18 α−0.31, with A and B for the grains' Stokes number (DSHARP VII's own choice per gap), h/r from a passively heated disc (T = (φ L*/8πr²σ)1/4, φ = 0.02). A gap is not proof of a planet: ice lines, magnetic winds and dust growth can make rings too.
