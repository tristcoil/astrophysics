Find the delay

A galaxy in front of a distant quasar bends its light into several images. Each image's light took a different path, so the same flicker arrives at different times. Slide one image's light curve in time over the reference image until the wiggles line up: that shift is the time delay. Delays like these, together with a model of the lens galaxy's mass, measure the expansion rate of the universe without any distance ladder.

Light curves: COSMOGRAIL (Millon et al. 2020a; Bonvin et al. 2018, 2019; Eulaers et al. 2013; Courbin et al. 2018), from VizieR. Telescope zero points and microlensing (a slow spline per image, relative to the reference) were removed with this project's joint fit; magnitudes relative to the reference image's median. Mismatch = variance of the magnitude difference between the shifted image and the reference, interpolated between reference points less than 12 days apart.
