    Temperatures we know

    The quark–gluon plasma temperatures are "effective" values from the photons it emits, which are blue-shifted by the plasma's flow (ALICE: 297 ± 12 ± 41 MeV; PHENIX: 221 ± 19 ± 19 MeV). An LHC proton carries 6.8 TeV; the average particle carried that much at the time shown.

    How it was made: ideal Fermi and Bose gases for every Standard Model particle (PDG 2026 masses), a gas of all 454 PDG hadrons below 120 MeV, the lattice-QCD equation of state of Borsányi et al. (2016) up to 1 GeV and perturbative QCD above; neutrino decoupling solved with Escudero's (2020) equations (Neff = ); the Friedmann equation integrated with Planck 2018's matter and dark energy. Checked against Saikawa & Shirai (2018), Laine, Meyer & Schröder (2015) and Borsányi et al. (2016).

    Ages count from the formal t = 0 of a hot, radiation-filled universe; whatever happened earlier (inflation) shifts them by far less than the first tick. The glow colour is that of a blackbody at the photon temperature. Before 372,000 years the universe was an opaque, glowing fog; after it, the leftover light was visible to human eyes until it fell below about 800 K.
