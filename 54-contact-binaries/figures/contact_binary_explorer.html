Two stars that share one envelope: choose their mass ratio, how far they overfill their Roche lobes, and the angle we see them from. The image is ray-traced from the Roche potential; the light curve is the sum of the image around the orbit. Lay it over real stars below.

How it is computed: Kopal's Roche potential for a circular, synchronised orbit; the surface is the equipotential with fill-out f between the inner (L1) and outer (L2) critical surfaces. Each pixel's ray is marched to the surface, where temperature follows gravity (T ∝ g0.08, Lucy 1967) from each star's polar temperature (5800 K for the heavier star), intensity is Planck's law at 640 nm (Kepler band) or 800 nm (OGLE I, chosen with the V1309 Sco data), with quadratic limb darkening. No starspots, no reflection: the real stars' unequal maxima (spots) are the part the model cannot reproduce. V1309 Sco preset: Zhu et al.'s q = 0.094 and f = 0.90; the inclination, 69°, is our match to the 2002 amplitude. The same code (roche.py) reproduces Kepler's KIC 9832227 amplitude to 0.002 mag with Molnar et al.'s parameters. Data: Kepler long cadence (our 14-harmonic template); OGLE-III I band (Tylenda et al. 2011), folded on our exponential ephemeris, magnitudes relative to each season's median.
