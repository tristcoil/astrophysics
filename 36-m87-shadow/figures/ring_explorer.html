Fit the black hole in M87 by hand

The Event Horizon Telescope never took a picture: each pair of telescopes measured one Fourier component of the sky. Shape a ring with the sliders and see, instantly, what the EHT would have recorded from it on the real baselines of April 2017, drawn over what it did record. A ring about 45 µas across puts the dip in the amplitudes where the data have it. Lopsidedness and its direction set the closure phases.

Data: EHT 2017 M87 release 2019-D01-01 (HOPS pipeline, low band, scan-averaged; intra-site baselines left out). The χ² uses the minimal set of closure phases with independent errors; the analysis behind the README uses their full covariance and richer models (a second brightness mode, a central floor and two extra Gaussian components), which is why "our fitted ring" does not reach χ²/N ≈ 1 here. Station gains are not modelled on this page, so amplitudes from the LMT and SMT can sit off by tens of percent. Mass: θ_g = d / α, M = θ_g D c² / G with D = 16.8 Mpc.
