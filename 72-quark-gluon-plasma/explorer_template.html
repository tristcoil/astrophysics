Freeze-out Thermometer

A heavy-ion collision melts protons and neutrons into a drop of free quarks and gluons, the stuff that filled the universe for its first ten microseconds. As the drop cools, the quarks bind into hadrons. How many of each kind come out depends only on one temperature and one baryon chemical potential. Move them, and every yield below is recomputed from a gas of 459 hadrons and light nuclei; or press Fit.

Data: ALICE (Pb–Pb, √sNN = 2.76 TeV, 0–10% central) and STAR (Au+Au, 0–5%) via HEPData and the papers. Model: ideal hadron resonance gas with quantum statistics, PDG 2025 hadron list (Thermal-FIST's tables), Breit–Wigner widths, strong and electromagnetic decays as each experiment defines its yields (STAR's protons include Λ decays), net strangeness zero and the nuclei's charge-to-baryon ratio. For ALICE, about ten charm–anticharm pairs add pions and kaons. Errors are statistical and systematic in quadrature; the volume is refitted at every step. The universe's age at each temperature is from project P2's calendar. Built for the Astrophysics Project Atlas (P6); every number here is reproduced by the project's Python code.
